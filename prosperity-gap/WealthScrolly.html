The Growing Prosperity Gap

America is a very rich country, with phenomenal wealth creation over time. But there have been big changes in which portions of the US population own that wealth. You probably know the rich are getting richer, and the rest, not so much. You might be surprised, however, by some of the details.

There’s always a wealth gap in a capitalist economy. But it has grown unusually wide during the last 40 years, and that’s probably one big reason consumer confidence is generally dismal.

Stocks · By wealth level

But Americans aren’t sharing that newfound wealth equally. Far from it.

Let’s start with stocks. The richest 10% of Americans own 87.4% of all stocks — up from 81.6% in 1989. The bottom 90% own just 12.6%, down from 18.3% in 1989.

Stocks · By age group

The baby boomers raked it in

Stocks have been the biggest wealth creator of the last four decades, and the wealthy have benefited disproportionately. The change has been even greater when you break it down by age. Americans 55 and over own 79.2% of stocks — up from 58.7% in 1989. Those under 55 own just 20.9%, down from 41.4% in 1989.

Younger Americans and those who aren’t already wealthy are benefiting from a roaring stock market less than they used to.

Real estate · By age group

A pernicious economic trend

There’s been a stark change in home ownership by age. Americans 55 and over own 61.2% of real estate wealth, up from 42.4% in 1989. Those under 55 own just 38.8%, down from 57.9% in 1989.

The shares owned by older and younger Americans reached parity in 2008 — the year of the financial crash and the Great Recession. Since then, the share of real estate owned by those under 55 has steadily declined. The falling homeownership rate among younger Americans is one of the most pernicious trends in the US economy.

Total net worth · The full picture

Lack of wealth is an affordability problem

“The affordability crisis” is a catchall phrase for the financial anxieties many Americans feel in 2026. But one huge underlying cause is that millions of families find it difficult or impossible to build wealth.

The richest 10% now own 68.3% of all household wealth — up from 60.8% in 1989. The bottom 90% own just 31.7%. By age, Americans 55 and over own 73.6% of all wealth, up from 56% in 1989.
