The Growing Prosperity Gap

America is a very rich country, with phenomenal wealth creation over time. But there have been big changes in which portions of the US population own that wealth. You probably know the rich are getting richer, and the rest, not so much. You might be surprised, however, by some of the details.

There’s always a wealth gap in a capitalist economy. But it has grown unusually wide during the last 40 years, and that’s probably one big reason consumer confidence is generally dismal.

Stock wealth, 1989–2025

The biggest single source of wealth is stocks, which account for 33% of total net worth. The value of stocks held by Americans exploded from $2.2 trillion in 1989 to $57.7 trillion in 2025 — a massive 2,580% gain.

2023–present →

The AI investment boom has driven the most dramatic surge in stock wealth in the data — adding more than $17 trillion in just two years.

Real estate wealth, 1989–2025

The next biggest source of wealth is real estate, accounting for 27% of net worth. The value of real estate jumped from $7 trillion in 1989 to $47.9 trillion in 2025. That’s a 585% increase.

2020 →

The Covid pandemic triggered a real-estate rush that sent prices soaring and pushed ownership out of reach for many.

But Americans aren’t sharing that newfound wealth equally. Far from it.

The richest 10% of Americans own 87.4% of all stocks — up from 81.6% in 1989. The bottom 90% own just 12.6%, down from 18.3% in 1989.

The baby boomers raked it in

Americans 55 and over own 79.2% of stocks — up from 58.7% in 1989. Those under 55 own just 20.9%, down from 41.4% in 1989. Younger Americans are benefiting from a roaring stock market less than they used to.

A pernicious economic trend

Americans 55 and over own 61.2% of real estate wealth, up from 42.4% in 1989. Those under 55 own just 38.8%, down from 57.9% in 1989.

The shares reached parity in 2008 — the year of the financial crash. Since then, the share owned by those under 55 has steadily declined. The falling homeownership rate among younger Americans is one of the most pernicious trends in the US economy.

Lack of wealth is an affordability problem

“The affordability crisis” is a catchall phrase for the financial anxieties many Americans feel in 2026. But one huge underlying cause is that millions of families find it difficult or impossible to build wealth.

The richest 10% now own 68.3% of all household wealth — up from 60.8% in 1989. The bottom 90% own just 31.7%. By age, Americans 55 and over own 73.6% of all wealth, up from 56% in 1989.
